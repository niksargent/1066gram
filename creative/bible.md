# 1066gram — Creative Bible

> *It's 1066. Everyone's posting.*

The Bayeux Tapestry reimagined as a social feed. You scroll; the Tapestry comes alive. Some posts talk, some open into a 3D world of wool, all of them are funny. One gesture (scroll), one reward (share).

---

## 1. Tone

**"Horrible Histories after the watershed."** Dry, quick, very British. Cheeky, never crude. No swearing.

The rules:

1. **Every joke points at something real.** The prefab castle, Odo's club, the quicksand rescue, the named-for-no-reason Turold — it's all in the Tapestry. Humour is the reason to look closer.
2. **Modern format, medieval content — never modern content.** Harold can post a run tracker; he cannot mention Brexit. The joke is the collision, not topical references.
3. **The comments are the comedy engine.** Main posts set up; comments pay off. Characters sniping at each other in replies is the most shareable thing we make.
4. **Underplay.** Deadpan beats wacky. A slow head-turn beats a scream.
5. **Two moments played completely straight:** the comet (awe) and Harold's death (quiet). Earned silence makes everything else funnier.
6. **Accuracy is loose.** True where truth is funnier. Invented where invention is better.

---

## 2. Visual direction — "The Paper Theatre"

- **Redrawn, not photographed.** Our own illustrations in the Tapestry's language.
- **Cut-out puppetry.** Characters are flat, jointed pieces (head, torso, arms, legs, props) that hinge, bob and gesture. Think Gilliam's Python animations meets a Victorian toy theatre. Stiffness *is* the comedy.
- **The eight wools**, on linen:
  - Terracotta red · Ochre / buff · Sage green · Dark blue-green · Indigo · Near-black blue · Grey-green · Linen ground
- **Stitch texture everywhere.** Stem-stitch outlines; laid-and-couched fills (visible parallel threads). Slight wobble. Nothing is perfectly straight.
- **Latin captions** in the Tapestry's lettering, above scenes, as in the original ("HIC…").
- **3D "Immersive" posts:** the linen lifts, the cut-outs stand up into a felt-and-thread diorama with depth, gentle camera drift, then folds back flat. Stop-motion feel — Aardman, not Assassin's Creed. Used **three times only**: the Comet, the Crossing, the Battle.
- **Animation rhythm:** small idle loops (breathing, bobbing, blinking via a stitched eyelid), occasional "to camera" looks.

### The feed UI

- Familiar vertical feed, re-skinned in linen and wool. Nobody needs to learn anything.
- Header: **1066gram** in stitched lettering.
- Story rings along the top = the cast. Tap for that character's profile.
- Likes are **stitched hearts**. Counts are absurd (`4.2M`) and in period units where funny ("liked by Stigand and 7,000 others").
- Verified tick = **a tiny crown**. It moves from Edward → Harold → William over the feed.
- Every post timestamped with its 1066 date. A **thread timeline** down the edge lets you jump anywhere. No locking — it's all available, always.
- **Sponsored** posts tagged as such. Comments expand under each post.
- Every post has its own link; sharing drops a friend onto that exact post.

## 7. Production notes

- **Voice budget:** about 3,500 words of script ≈ 20,000 characters. With three takes of each line, ≈ 60,000 characters. Well within 200k.
- **Takes:** generate 3 takes per line and choose the best. Comic timing lives in the pauses — choose for timing over polish.
- **Extra audio to make** (also via ElevenLabs, sound effects): sea, wind, crowd murmur, war horns, horse whinnies (Gerald), bell, sizzle, thwacks, cheering, distant battle. A soft lute-and-drone theme for the feed (or licensed/royalty-free).
- **Audio defaults:** muted until the first tap (browser rule); after that, reels autoplay with sound as they enter the screen, like any feed.
- **No public AI.** Everything is pre-made. Get Stitched is templates and typed text only.
